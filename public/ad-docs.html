How It Works

Wordeth ads use contextual keyword matching to connect your brand with relevant music content. When a user searches for lyrics, our system checks for ads with matching keywords and displays them alongside the results.

1

Create Your Account

Register for an advertiser account through our registration page. Choose between Self-Serve or Partner (White Glove) account types.

2

Design Your Ad

Create your ad creative following our size specifications. Upload your image and set your click-through URL.

3

Choose Your Keywords

Select up to 25 keywords that relate to your brand. Your ad will appear when users search for lyrics containing these terms.

4

Submit for Review

Our team reviews all ads within 24-48 hours. Once approved, your ad goes live automatically.

Keyword Targeting

Keyword targeting is how your ads reach the right audience. Your ad is shown when a user searches for lyrics that match one or more of your keywords.

How Keyword Matching Works

When a user searches for a song or lyrics, our system checks their search query against your keyword list. If there's a match, your ad is eligible to appear alongside the results.

Keyword Examples

Ice Cream Brand

ice cream, summer, sweet, cold, treat, dessert, frozen, vanilla, chocolate, strawberry

These keywords would match searches for songs about summer, sweets, or treats.

Headphone Company

music, beats, sound, bass, listen, headphones, audio, volume, rhythm, vibe

Music-related terms naturally appear in many song searches.

Streetwear Brand

style, fresh, drip, fashion, flex, swag, outfit, wear, cool, trend

Slang and culture-related terms that appear in hip-hop and pop lyrics.

Keyword Tips

  • Use up to 25 keywords per ad
  • Think about words that naturally appear in song lyrics
  • Include product names, related themes, and common associations
  • Mix broad terms (e.g., "music") with specific ones (e.g., "headphones")
  • Consider genre-specific slang and terminology
  • Separate keywords with commas

Best Practices

Follow these guidelines to create effective ads that engage Wordeth's music-loving audience.

Use High-Quality Images

Clear, professional images grab attention. Avoid pixelated or stretched graphics. Make sure your brand is easily recognizable.

Include a Clear Call to Action

Tell users what you want them to do: "Shop Now," "Learn More," "Get 20% Off." A strong CTA drives higher click-through rates.

Match Your Landing Page

Make sure your click-through URL leads to a page relevant to your ad. Don't send users to a generic homepage if your ad promotes a specific product.

Choose Relevant Keywords

Pick keywords that genuinely relate to your brand. Irrelevant keywords waste impressions and annoy users.

Keep Text Minimal

Banner ads have limited space. Use short, punchy copy that communicates your message at a glance.

Consider Your Audience

Wordeth users are music enthusiasts. Ads that connect with music culture tend to perform better.

Pausing, resuming and the on-ramp

What happens when I pause an ad?

It stops being shown straight away, in every placement, and nothing is charged while it is paused. Its artwork, keywords, impressions, clicks and spend to date are all kept.

What happens when I start it again?

It is back in the running immediately, but it re-enters on the on-ramp. It starts at a quarter of its normal weight and climbs steadily to full weight over 24 hours. During that day it is shown less often than an established ad with the same fit and the same bid, and the gap closes hour by hour.

Will my ad show at all while it is on the on-ramp?

Yes. The on-ramp only changes how a slot is shared between ads competing for the same moment. If yours is the only ad that fits, or it fits better than the others, it is shown every time from the first minute.

How do ads compete for a slot?

Fit comes first. Only the ads whose keywords best match what the person is doing are considered at all. When several fit equally well, they share the slot in proportion to their bid (the rate per thousand impressions) multiplied by their on-ramp strength. A higher bid is shown more often; it does not take every impression.

An example: two ads fit a search equally and carry the same bid. One has been running all week and the other was resumed a moment ago. The established ad is shown about four times in five and the resumed one about once in five. Twelve hours later the split is roughly 60 to 40, and after 24 hours it is even.

Why is there an on-ramp?

So that ads already running are not pushed aside the moment a competitor presses Resume, and so that stopping and starting an ad is never a way to jump ahead of other advertisers. Ads that stay on hold their ground.

Does a brand-new ad go through the on-ramp?

Yes. Its 24 hours begin when it is approved, or when it is created if it goes live at once.

Does running out of budget put my ad back on the on-ramp?

It depends on which budget. Budgets are optional caps set on a campaign with the Wordeth team. An ad that reaches its daily budget stops for the rest of that day and returns the next day at the strength it had; that is not a restart. An ad that reaches its total budget is paused, so when you raise the budget and resume it, it re-enters on the on-ramp. If a prepaid account runs out of balance, its ads stop and return at their existing strength once the account is topped up.

Does editing my ad restart the on-ramp?

No. Changing the title, description, link, keywords or rate leaves an ad where it is. Only starting an ad starts the on-ramp.

Am I charged differently during the on-ramp?

No. You pay for the impressions and clicks actually delivered, at your normal rate. An ad on the on-ramp is delivered less against competitors, so it spends less during that day.

Should I pause an ad for a short break?

If other advertisers are competing for the same audience, a short pause costs you a day of reduced share when you return. To slow an ad down without leaving the race, ask us to lower its daily budget instead.
